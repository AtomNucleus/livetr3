## Sermon error ranking

I took sermon-v1 and sermon-v2 from three cache-on passes (3c-screen, B1 and
B2) and aligned them with `fleurs_score`'s tokenizer (`rank_errors.py`). That
gives about 51 English word errors per pass. Most recur in all three passes,
so they are systematic rather than noise. No class dominates:

| Class | Errors per pass | Examples | Fixable in general? |
| --- | ---: | --- | --- |
| Short silence-ended captions without context (≤4 words) | ≈5 | "Rains" for "reigns", "Rather graces the free" for "rather, grace is the free", "Born a savior", "but now remain" | Tried below |
| One-off homophones and word forms inside long captions | ≈20 | man→men, establish→established, prove→approve, rancor→ranker | No general lever |
| Function words dropped or normalized by the model | ≈10 | "told to their lord" → "told their lord", "due to him" | The prompt already says to preserve every word |
| Scripture-reference format | ≈3 | "Philippians chapter 4 verse 10 through 13" vs reference "4:10-13" | Appears once in all the references; a rewrite would overfit the eval |
| Reference typos | ≈1 | reference "versus" where the speaker said "verses" | Not a model error |

By final length (three passes):

| Final length | Finals | Word error rate |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| 1–2 words | 32 | 6.2% |
| 3–4 words | 51 | 6.9% |
| 5–8 words | 191 | 3.5% |
| 9+ words | 301 | 2.1% |

Short finals are just as common live: in the user's last 79 sessions, 5% of
2,297 finals had 1–2 words, all silence-ended, and 5% had 3–4.

I also checked a hypothesis about lost words. The segmenter drops
silence-ended fragments shorter than 800 ms. An offline run over all three
sets (`dropped_fragments.py`, `decode_dropped.py`) found 35 such fragments.
All are near-silent, with RMS around 0.0003, and Gemma decodes every one as
"No, I don't know." So dropping them is correct, and that hallucination never
reaches a real final.

## Fix 1: join a lone short utterance to the next — rejected

With `LIVETR3_SHORT_JOIN_MS=1800`, a silence-ended utterance shorter than
1.8 s waits up to `LIVETR3_SHORT_JOIN_HOLD_MS` (800 ms) for speech to resume.
If it resumes, the next words join the same utterance.

The first offline version also stretched sub-800 ms fragments past the minimum
length. FLEURS then committed 15 near-silent fragments that are normally
dropped. Holding now starts only for utterances that already meet the
minimum.

Offline on sermon-v2, it held 12 utterances and joined 8. The screening pass
on the full stack:

| Pass | Live-weighted median / p95 | sermon-v2 WER | Pooled WER | 1–2-word finals per pass |
| --- | --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| cache-on passes (screen, B1, B2) | 1.49–1.55 / 2.46–2.53 | 2.46–2.62% | 4.75–4.84% | ≈11 |
| 4a-join1800 | 1.46 / 2.31 | 2.62% | 4.84% | 4 |

It does what it was built to do: lone 1–2-word finals fell from about 11 to 4.
But the errors it was aimed at stay. "Rather graces the free", the lone
"Grace" and "Born a savior" are unchanged, and sermon-v2 WER stays inside the
range of the other passes. Those lone words follow pauses of 3 to 7.7 s, far
beyond any hold that wouldn't add real latency.

The latency gain is not this change's doing. Most of it is on FLEURS, where
only 2 utterances were joined.

Rejected. The code is parked on the branch `claude/short-join`, with no PR.

There is no second fix. None of the remaining classes has a general lever
that wouldn't just fit the references.

Across all sets, live-weighted final latency drops by **0.15 s at the median
and 0.12 s at p95**. First target is 0.17 s sooner and gap p95 is 0.13 s
better. Every after pass beats both before passes on all of these.

Quality is flat:
- pooled WER +0.05 pt, entirely from FLEURS (+0.11 pt, 1–2 of its 1,363
  words); both sermon sets are identical
- chrF++ unchanged
- numbers 2/51 and negation 1/55 in every pass
- no dropped finals

sermon-v1's first target, 3.91 s after vs 3.48 and 3.51 s before, is wording
drift, not a slower caption. Spanish actually appears sooner after: 0.88 s vs
0.97 s. But the opening previews keep "Paul confiesa…" while the final says
"Pablo…", and first target counts only preview words that survive into the
final. Its first hit therefore moves later. This is the cache's greedy-path
numeric drift, as in the 3c identity check.
